Squat cue: “hips back, chest forward”

The cue “sit back, chest up” often creates two issues:

People chase “chest up” by changing pelvic/spinal position instead of staying neutrally braced.

People try to stay too upright while sitting back, shifting balance behind midfoot, then compensating late in the descent to avoid tipping.

Accept that some forward torso lean is normal in a squat. Forcing upright posture can push you into worse positions.

A simpler cue that tends to work better: push hips back while reaching the chest forward.

Mental model: imagine a vertical “wall” at your knees or midfoot.

Descend by sending hips back while the chest “slides forward” along that wall the whole way down.

Goal is a smooth, natural increase in hip flexion while keeping the system balanced over midfoot.

Benefit: can replace multiple cues with one and improve consistency of positioning.

Pause squats: overlooked benefits

Most people know pause squats can help with depth, bottom control, and limiting/cleaning technique. Additional useful benefits:

Less dependence on a perfect rebound

A fast eccentric + big bounce can be a technique skill, but it can also be fragile.

If a small error at the bottom causes a big strength drop, pauses help you develop the ability to stand up from an isometric bottom position.

Lifters with strong pause squats often recover better from slightly “off” positions because they don’t rely on an ideal rebound.

Foot/ankle strength and deep-position control

Holding a real pause at depth exposes you to deep ankle dorsiflexion while you maintain arch and ankle position.

Even if the intent is bracing/control, you often get stronger and more stable in the foot/ankle at the deepest position.

Longer pauses (e.g., ~3 seconds) on lighter/technique days can emphasize this.

Potentially easier on knees vs aggressive rebound

Heavy, rebound-dependent squatting can be rough on the knees for some lifters, especially with very high specificity and frequency.

Pauses reduce reliance on elastic rebound and may reduce the stress of the fast reversal.

Used intelligently across a training year, pause work can support squat strength while improving long-term tolerance.

Pelvic position in the squat: stop “glute squeezing” at the top

Many lifters try to set neutral pelvis by hard glute squeezing at the top.

Problem: glutes must lengthen as you descend, so they can’t stay “locked” in that squeezed position.

What often happens:

lifter squeezes hard

then immediately “pops” into the opposite pelvic position as the glutes un-contract and stretch

Better approach: control pelvis with the brace, not glute clenching.

Use abdominals and obliques to create a stable, neutral position that can stay isometric through the rep.

Simple demo:

stand tall and forcefully exhale

abs/obliques contract and the pelvis naturally tucks slightly

that’s closer to the mechanism you want: trunk muscles holding pelvic position

Coaching preference:

start with “soft hips” + neutral brace

then drop hips down while allowing knees to travel forward as needed

this reduces the instinct to start the squat with a big hip-pop back
