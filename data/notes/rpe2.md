Best RPE for gaining strength

Key takeaway

For strength-focused training, the article’s practical recommendation is that most work should sit around ~RPE 5–8.

Reason: pushing closer to failure tends to increase fatigue without showing a clear extra strength payoff once load is accounted for.

Source: Stronger By Science (Milo Wolf, Feb 5, 2025)

What the recommendation is based on

1) Meta-analysis / meta-regression (55 studies)

Robinson et al. pooled the proximity-to-failure literature and, in a load-adjusted analysis, found no clear relationship between “how close to failure” and strength gains.

Why “load-adjusted” matters:

Higher RPE usually allows heavier loads for the same rep target.

Heavier loads do tend to improve maximal strength.

But this model statistically controls for load, so it isolates the “closeness to failure” question.

Source: Stronger By Science summary of the meta-analysis

2) Fatigue tradeoff

Training closer to failure is generally more fatiguing.

If strength gains are similar across RPE levels (after accounting for load), then higher RPE becomes a higher-cost approach with no consistent benefit.

Source: Stronger By Science interpretation

3) Follow-up intervention study (4 groups)

Well-trained lifters followed the same sets/reps, but training differed by proximity to failure / %1RM.

Groups:

Group 1: 4–6 reps in reserve (intended), but in practice trained even more submaximally than planned.

Group 2: 1–3 reps in reserve

Group 3: same as Group 2, but final set to failure

Group 4: every set to failure (RPE 10)

Outcome summary reported:

Strength gains in squat + bench were highest in Groups 1 and 2

Groups training closer to failure progressed more slowly

Fatigue was generally highest in the all-to-failure group

Source: Stronger By Science summary of the study

Practical rules to store/use

Default strength zone

Put most “strength work” at ~RPE 5–8:

hard enough for stimulus and meaningful loads

far enough from failure to manage fatigue and recovery

Source: Stronger By Science conclusion

When to go closer to failure

Off-season / hypertrophy emphasis: some closer-to-failure work can make sense to drive muscle gain, supporting long-term strength.

Near competition: include some high-RPE singles (or heavy sets of 2–4 reps) for specificity and practice handling/grinding heavy weights.

Source: Stronger By Science recommendations
